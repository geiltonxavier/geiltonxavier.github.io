About

I'm Geilton.

I was born in Ilhéus, on the southern coast of Bahia, and moved to São Paulo when I was eight years old. Looking back, that move probably shaped the way I think about systems: adaptation, resilience, and learning new environments became normal long before I wrote my first line of code.

I didn't enter technology through architecture. I started in support, ERP implementations, SQL fixes, user training, and the kind of operational work where every bug has a person waiting on the other side. That experience taught me that software is rarely the real problem. Friction is.

Today I'm a Solutions Architect and hands-on technical lead, designing distributed systems on .NET and Azure. Most of my work sits at the intersection of engineering and organizations, building platforms, identity systems, and developer tooling that help hundreds of engineers move faster without sacrificing reliability.

I'm particularly interested in platform engineering, event-driven architecture, cloud infrastructure, AI-assisted software development, and the craft of making complex systems feel simple. The projects I'm proudest of are often the least visible: internal platforms, automation, engineering standards, and infrastructure that quietly removes thousands of repetitive decisions from people's daily work.

I still write code every week because I believe architecture is a practice, not a title. The best design decisions come from staying close to the implementation.

Outside work, I build small products, write about engineering, and spend an unreasonable amount of time thinking about how developers learn. I enjoy creating things that outlast the excitement of their first release.
